# Competitors

Who else sells this, what they charge, and where we genuinely differ.

Prices from the providers' own pages, August 2026, at ₹95.5/USD. Sources at the bottom.

> **Read §3 before §2.** Per wedding, we are three to eighteen times more expensive than everyone

## 1. The field

Three groups, and only one of them is really aiming at the same thing.

### Photo-first client galleries — the incumbents

Where photographers already live. Video is a bolt-on and the allowances show it.

| | Entry | Storage | **Video allowance** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pixieset** | $10/mo | 10 GB | **30 minutes** |
| Pixieset Plus | $20/mo | 100 GB | **1 hour** |
| Pixieset Pro | $50/mo | 1 TB | **2 hours** |
| Pixieset Ultimate | $50/mo | unlimited | **5 hours** |
| **ShootProof** | $10–30/mo | by photo count (1.5k–25k) | secondary |
| **Pic-Time** | $10–26/mo | by photo count | secondary |
| **CloudSpot** | ~$19/mo | varies | secondary |
| **SmugMug** | ~$13/mo | unlimited | unlimited 4K, but a photo host |

**The number that matters: Pixieset's most expensive plan holds five hours of video.** A 10–15
hour Indian wedding does not fit *any* Pixieset plan at any price. ShootProof and Pic-Time price
by photo count, so video is barely modelled at all.

These platforms are not weak at video by accident. They are built for a photographer delivering
600 images and a highlights reel.

### Wedding-video delivery — the direct competitors

| | Price | Video storage | Limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **OurStoria Pro** | $24.99/mo | 450 GB | 40 projects, **10 videos each** |
| **OurStoria Studio** | $59.99/mo | 1.5 TB | 120 projects, **15 videos each** |
| **VidFlow** | $15+/mo | scales | white-label galleries |
| **MediaZilla** | — | — | markets on lifetime client access |

OurStoria is the closest thing to a direct competitor: white-label on every tier, photo *and*
video, guest uploads ("Live Moments"), a CRM, and a per-project archive add-on at **$12–19/year**
so a gallery survives the studio cancelling.

Their per-project video cap of 10–15 files is the tell. It fits a Western wedding — a film, a
teaser, a ceremony — and is tight for eight functions across two sides.

### White-label OTT — the wrong shape

Uscreen and Vimeo OTT price **per subscriber per month** ($1–2) or a flat plan in the high
hundreds. Built for creators running a subscription business, not for one wedding watched by two
hundred people once. Wrong economics, wrong workflow, not a real competitor.

### And the actual incumbent

**Google Drive, WeTransfer and a hard disk.** Free or nearly, universally understood, and what
most Indian studios use today. Every platform here is really competing with it.

## 3. The uncomfortable finding

**We are 3–18× more expensive per wedding than every competitor in this document.**

That is not a rounding difference to be argued away. It is a different model, and the difference
is worth being precise about:

| | Them | **Us** |
|---|---|---|
| Who pays | The **studio**, monthly, forever | The **couple**, once, via the studio |
| What it is to the studio | **Overhead** | **A product they resell** |
| Studio's margin on it | None — it is a cost | **57–69%** |
| What happens if they stop paying | **Every gallery goes dark** | The wedding is the couple's; it renews or is deleted |
| Capacity for 15 hours | No | Yes |

**The strategic risk is framing, not price.** Pixieset and OurStoria have spent a decade training
studios to think of delivery as a monthly overhead — a line item to minimise. A studio in that
frame hears "₹7,000 per wedding" against "₹955" and stops listening, and no feature list recovers
the conversation.

A studio in the other frame hears "sell this to your couple for ₹20,000 and keep ₹13,000", which
is not a cost at all.

**Everything depends on which conversation you are having**, and it is the same thing
`PRICING.md` §6 flags: your own market said ₹5,000, and the suggested retail is 3–4× that. This
document is the evidence that the question is real, not the answer to it.

### What would settle it

Ask the studio owner two questions, in this order:

1. *"What do you currently pay to deliver a wedding, and to whom?"* — if the answer is "nothing, I
   use Drive", the comparison above is not in his head at all and you are selling against free.
2. *"Would you put a line on your quotation for the couple's private streaming site?"* — if yes,
   you are a product. If he flinches, you are overhead, and the ladder needs halving.

## 4. Where we genuinely win

Four things, and only the first two are hard to copy.

**Capacity for an Indian wedding.** Nobody else fits 10–15 hours across eight functions from both
sides. Pixieset tops out at five hours. OurStoria caps files per project at ten to fifteen. This is
not a feature gap they have overlooked — it is that the market they built for does not have
sangeet, haldi and mehendi as separate multi-hour films.

**A catalogue, not a gallery.** Everyone here delivers a grid of files behind a password. This
delivers a browsable page with a billboard, rows, a written letter, a timeline, adaptive streaming
that starts in under a second and a half on 4G, resume-where-you-left-off, and a profile gate — no
account, no login, two hundred guests from a WhatsApp link. That is a materially different thing
to be sent, and it is the reason a couple forwards it.

**The couple owns it.** Every competitor ties the gallery to the studio's subscription: stop
paying and it goes dark. Handover moves the wedding into the couple's own account, and they renew
directly. Only OurStoria's Safe Archive (**$12–19/year**, ₹1,146–1,814) does anything comparable —
and it is cold storage for downloading, not a live site. **Our renewal at ₹2,499 is roughly
priced against it**, which is worth knowing.

**Built for the market.** English and Hindi throughout, Mumbai region, sub-1.5s playback on
mid-range Android over 4G, and pricing in rupees. Every platform above is a US product with an
Indian invoice.

## 5. Where we lose, and it is not close

**Price per wedding**, as above.

**Everything around the delivery.** ShootProof and Pixieset bundle contracts, invoicing, proofing,
client CRM and a print store. OurStoria has a CRM. **We have none of it.** For a studio choosing
one tool, that is a real argument, and building any of it is a different product.

**Photo workflow.** There is no proofing, no favouriting, no selection, no client feedback loop,
no print sales. Our photo support is "an album on the wedding page". A photographer's core
workflow is not here at all — which is fine while the customer is a *videographer*, and fatal if
you chase photographers.

**Track record.** Pixieset has been around since 2013. We have one live catalogue.

**Nothing is bundled.** They sell a subscription that covers thirty weddings. We sell thirty
things. That is more invoices, more decisions, and more chances to say no.

---

## 6. What this suggests

Three things, in confidence order.

**1. Never compete on price per wedding.** You will lose by 3–18×. Compete on what a couple
receives and what a studio can charge for it. The sentence is *"your couple pays you ₹20,000 for
this"*, never *"it costs ₹7,000"*.

**2. Sell against Google Drive, not against Pixieset.** Most Indian studios are on Drive and a
hard disk. Against Drive we are obviously better and the price is a new line rather than a
comparison. Against Pixieset we are a premium alternative in a category they defined — a harder
conversation and a smaller pool.

**3. Capacity is the wedge, and it is defensible.** "Every function, both sides, fifteen hours" is
a sentence no competitor can currently answer, and it is not a feature they can ship next quarter
— it is a consequence of what their pricing assumes a wedding to be. Lead with it.

### Two things to watch

**OurStoria's Safe Archive at $12–19/year is the model to beat on renewals**, not the monthly
plan. It is cheap, permanent-feeling, and solves the same anxiety our renewal does.

**If a competitor ships a "long-form wedding" tier, the wedge closes.** The moat is not the
technology — Bunny is available to everyone. It is that we designed for a fifteen-hour wedding
from the start and they would have to reprice to follow.
