# Pricing model

What we sell, what it costs us, and why.

Costs are from the providers' live pricing pages, August 2026. Every figure is worked rather than
asserted so you can redo it when a rate moves. Sources at the bottom.

> This is a business model, not advice about your finances. The numbers are arithmetic on
> published rates; the judgement calls are marked as judgement calls.

## The answer, in one page

**You sell one thing: storage.** Everything else follows from it.

| Plan | Storage | **To partner** | They sell for | Renewal /yr |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Highlights** | 10 GB | **₹2,500** | ₹6,000–8,000 | ₹999 |
| **Signature** | 20 GB | **₹4,000** | ₹10,000–12,000 | ₹1,499 |
| **Full Wedding** | 40 GB | **₹6,500** | ₹15,000–20,000 | ₹2,499 |

Twelve months. Add storage any time at **₹25/GB/month for the months left**.

**Quality up to 1080p is free and unlimited — it just changes how many hours fit.**

| Quality | Storage per hour | 10 GB | 20 GB | **40 GB** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Standard** — up to 720p | 2.15 GB | 4.7 hrs | 9.3 hrs | **18.6 hrs** |
| **Full HD** — up to 1080p | 4.29 GB | 2.3 hrs | 4.7 hrs | 9.3 hrs |

**Cinema 2K / 4K** is a paid add-on, per film: **₹1,999 per 20 minutes.**

That is the whole product. Two numbers on a quotation — a plan and, if they want it, a cinema
finish.

**You never count hours.** Storage is the meter, the platform already measures it, and the
customer trades quality against runtime inside the space they bought. That trade is theirs, not
something you police.

## 1. Why quality is not a plan tier

You asked for tiers on two axes. It turns out **one of them is free**, which makes the product
much simpler than either of us expected.

Bunny's standard encoding — **free, no per-minute charge** — covers everything up to 1080p. So
720p and 1080p cost us *nothing* to produce. The only difference is how much space the result
occupies:

| Quality | Ladder stored | Per finished hour |
|---|---|---|
| Standard (to 720p) | 360p + 480p + 720p | **2.15 GB** |
| Full HD (to 1080p) | + 1080p | **4.29 GB** |

**1080p is twice the storage for the same money.** So a customer on 40 GB can have 18.6 hours at
720p or 9.3 hours at 1080p, and they should decide which. Charging extra for it would be charging
for something that costs us nothing, and it would need a second axis on every price list.

**2K and 4K are genuinely different**, because they need *premium* encoding, billed per minute of
video:

| | Cost |
|---|---|
| Standard encoding, up to 1080p | **Free** |
| Premium encoding, 4K package | **$0.15 per minute** |

A whole 15-hour wedding in 4K would cost **₹12,892 in encoding alone**, before storage, against a
₹6,500 plan. It cannot be a plan tier at any price a studio would pay. But 20 minutes of it costs
₹286 — so it works beautifully as a per-film add-on.

### What to tell the studio

> "Everything is 1080p-capable at no extra cost. Shoot the long functions at 720p and you fit
> eighteen hours in the top plan; put the ones people will rewatch in Full HD. If you want a 4K
> master of the highlights film, that is ₹1,999."

### The default, and this matters

**Set Standard (720p) as the default.** At Full HD a 15-hour wedding needs 64 GB and does not fit
any plan you sell. At Standard it is 32 GB and fits the 40 GB tier with room for a 4K highlights
film on top:

> 15 hrs at 720p (32.2 GB) + a 20-min 4K highlights film (4.3 GB) = **36.5 GB** ✓

## 2. Cinema 2K and 4K

Sold per film, in 20-minute blocks, because that is the unit a studio thinks in.

| | Price | Our cost | Margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cinema finish, one film to 20 min** | **₹1,999** | ₹286 | 86% |
| Each further 20 min | ₹1,499 | ₹215 | 86% |

2K and 4K are the **same price** — let them pick. The encoding rate is the same and 2K actually
uses less of their storage, so there is no reason to make them choose on price.

**It uses their plan's storage**, and you should say so: 20 minutes of 4K is 4.3 GB. That is the
honest version and it prevents the "why did my space disappear" call.

**The upsell writes itself.** A studio charges the couple ₹3,000–5,000 for a 4K master of the
highlights film. It costs them ₹1,999 and you ₹286.

> **One thing to confirm before publishing.** Bunny lists premium packages as SD / Full HD / 4K.
> 1440p sits above Full HD so I have costed it at the 4K rate. If it bills under Full HD
> ($0.05/min) your 2K margin improves and you might price 2K lower to make it the popular choice.

## 3. Adding storage

> **₹25 per GB per month, charged only for the months left on the plan.**

Co-terminus — it expires with the plan, so there is one renewal date and one invoice forever.

| Bought | Months left | They pay | Costs us |
|---|---|---|---|
| +2 GB | 11 | **₹550** | ₹21 |
| +5 GB | 9 | **₹1,125** | ₹43 |
| +10 GB | 6 | **₹1,500** | ₹57 |

Deliberately **more per GB than upgrading a tier** (₹300/GB/year against ₹125–150). Not a trap — a
signpost:

| Months left | Add-ons cheaper below | Above that |
|---|---|---|
| 12 | 5 GB | Upgrade the tier |
| 9 | 6.7 GB | Upgrade the tier |
| 6 | 10 GB | Upgrade the tier |

Small top-ups are cheap; big ones push to the next tier — which is what you want, because the tier
renews and the add-on does not.

**Rules:** whole GB only · never below what is stored · no refund on unused capacity, said up
front · a tier upgrade credits unused add-on months, so the signpost never becomes a penalty for
guessing wrong early.

## 4. Renewing, upgrading, downgrading

### Renewal — priced per tier

| Plan | Renewal | Costs us | Margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 GB | **₹999** | ₹149 | 85% |
| 20 GB | **₹1,499** | ₹263 | 82% |
| 40 GB | **₹2,499** | ₹492 | 80% |

Roughly 40% of the new-plan price. Year one includes setting the whole thing up; year two is
hosting, and the traffic burst has passed.

Charged to whoever owns the catalogue — after handover, the couple. Offer the partner **15% of
renewals they originated**: ₹150–375 a year, and the only thing that makes them set the couple's
expectations properly at handover, which is what actually drives renewal.

### Upgrading mid-term

**The difference, prorated by months remaining. No fee.**

> 10 → 20 GB with 7 months left: (₹4,000 − ₹2,500) × 7/12 = **₹875**

Never refuse or delay this — it always happens mid-delivery with a couple waiting.

### Downgrading — yes, at renewal

**This is the answer to your question: they can, but only at renewal, and only if the content
fits.**

| When | Allowed? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Mid-term | **No** | They have already used the capacity for those months. Offer a smaller renewal instead. |
| **At renewal** | **Yes** | New year, new commitment. |

The one condition is arithmetic, not policy: **stored content must fit the smaller plan.**

> "You are using 27 GB. To renew at 20 GB, remove 7 GB first — or renew at 40 GB."

This is why renewal is priced per tier. If renewal were a flat fee, downgrading would save them
nothing and the option would be meaningless.

**A couple's second year is exactly when this happens.** The wedding is 18 months old, they have
watched the long functions once, and they want to keep the highlights film and the album. Letting
them delete down to 10 GB and pay ₹999 keeps a customer who would otherwise cancel. **A downgrade
is a save, not a loss** — and the alternative is not a full-price renewal, it is deletion.

### What the console needs to show

Before any of this is sellable, the renewal screen has to answer one question: *what would I have
to delete?* It should show current usage against each tier, so downgrading is a decision rather
than a support ticket.

## 5. Break-even, explained plainly

You asked what I meant. Without the jargon:

**Some costs happen whether you sell one wedding or five hundred** — database and hosting,
₹51,570/year. Fixed.

**Every wedding brings in money and takes some away.** Full Wedding brings ₹6,500 and costs about
₹685 for that specific wedding. The ₹5,815 left over is what that sale **contributes** toward the
₹51,570.

**Break-even is how many sales until those contributions cover the fixed cost.**

> ₹51,570 ÷ ₹5,815 = **8.9 weddings**

| Weddings sold | Contributions | Where you stand |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | ₹17,445 | −₹34,125 |
| 6 | ₹34,890 | −₹16,680 |
| **9** | ₹52,335 | **+₹765 — level** |
| 15 | ₹87,225 | +₹35,655 |
| 30 | ₹1,74,450 | +₹1,22,880 |

Cheaper plans contribute less, so they take more:

| Plan | Contribution | Break-even |
|---|---|---|
| Highlights | ₹2,159 | **23.9 weddings** |
| Signature | ₹3,544 | **14.6 weddings** |
| Full Wedding | ₹5,815 | **8.9 weddings** |

**The one decision this drives:** lead with Full Wedding. Not to squeeze anyone — it is genuinely
what a 10–15 hour wedding needs — but the side effect is profit in 9 sales instead of 24.

**And the escape hatch:** that ₹51,570 is optional today. **Supabase Free and Vercel Hobby carry
this to roughly 50 weddings**, where the real fixed cost is Bunny's $1/month minimum — ₹1,146/year,
and you are in profit on **wedding #1**. Move to paid tiers when you hit a real limit, not on
principle.

PoPs globally, and the promise is playback starting in under a second and a half on 4G.

### Per wedding

| Plan | Storage/yr | Typical delivery | **Total** | Contribution |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 GB | ₹115 | ₹227 | **₹341** | ₹2,159 (86%) |
| 20 GB | ₹229 | ₹227 | **₹456** | ₹3,544 (89%) |
| 40 GB | ₹458 | ₹227 | **₹685** | ₹5,815 (90%) |

Delivery assumes 300 guests × 20 minutes at 1.8 Mbps. **The gap between the 10 GB and 40 GB plans
costs ₹343 a year; the gap between a quiet wedding and a flaunted one costs ₹1,048 on the same
plan.** You are selling the axis that barely varies.

### Bounding the one that does

| Plan | Delivery allowance | Worst case | Roughly |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 GB | 150 GB | ₹430 | ~570 guests |
| 20 GB | 300 GB | ₹860 | ~1,140 guests |
| 40 GB | 600 GB | ₹1,719 | ~2,280 guests |

**Past the allowance, do not bill automatically — call them.** A catalogue over its allowance is
either being loved, which is the best sales story you will get, or a leaked link, which they need
to know about. The margins say you can afford the conversation.

> The app meters storage but not delivery (`getUsage` returns `deliveredGb: 0`). The allowance is
> watched from the Bunny dashboard today.

## 7. Never sell this as storage

Your pitch was *"instead of Google Drive, store it with us — we have a player and it looks
better."* **The second half wins deals. The first half loses them.**

| | Price | Per GB/year |
|---|---|---|
| Google One, 2 TB | ₹6,500/yr | **₹3.17** |
| Our 40 GB | ₹6,500/yr | **₹162.50** |

**Same money, 51× less storage.** A studio owner who buys 2 TB drives for a living does that
arithmetic in his head, and you become a very expensive Drive.

What you actually sell: **a guest with no account opens a link on their phone at a reception, and
a film starts playing in under a second and a half, on a page carrying the couple's name and
colours.** Drive cannot do that at any price — no adaptive streaming, no branding, no resume, no
catalogue, and a link people can copy the file from.

**Practical rules:**

- **Never publish a per-GB headline price.** Say *"40 GB — the whole wedding, about 18 hours"*,
  never *"40 GB at ₹162/GB"*. The add-on rate is fine as a mechanism; nobody compares a ₹550
  top-up to Google One.
- **Refuse raw footage.** If a studio wants to park 200 GB of ungraded multicam, that is a backup
  service, you would be reselling storage at 51× Google's price, and the first invoice ends the
  relationship. *"Keep the rushes on Drive. Put the finished films here, where the family will
  actually watch them."*

## 8. Before you quote anyone

1. **Set the default encoding ladder to 360p–720p**, and offer 1080p as a per-film choice. At Full
   HD everywhere, a 15-hour wedding needs 64 GB and fits nothing you sell.
2. **Confirm Keep Original Files and MP4 Fallback are off.** Each roughly doubles storage.
3. **Upload one real 15-hour wedding and read the actual GB** off the catalogue overview — the
   product already meters real stored bytes. That turns every estimate here into a measurement.
4. **Confirm which premium package 1440p bills under**, so the 2K price is right.
5. **Hold the positioning line** — the guest experience, never the gigabytes.

Then build, in this order: per-film quality choice at upload (Bunny needs resolutions set *before*
upload), storage add-ons, renewal with a downgrade path, and billing. All of it is N-20 in
`docs/NEXT.md`; none of it exists yet. **Until it does, sell annual plans and invoice manually** —
with three tiers and a few partners, a spreadsheet is cheaper than billing you might price wrong.
